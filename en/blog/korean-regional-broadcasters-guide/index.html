What Are Korean Regional Broadcasters? A Local Channel Guide

Updated: September 2026

Browsing the channel list on korea-tv.uk, you'll notice broadcasters with regional names attached — MBC Jeju, MBC Chungbuk, KBC Gwangju, UBC Ulsan, OBS Gyeonggi. Here's what these regional broadcasters are, and how they differ from nationwide networks like KBS, MBC, and SBS.

What is a regional broadcaster

Korean terrestrial TV is often structured as a network with a Seoul-based headquarters (the "key station") and regional affiliates across the country. MBC, for example, has regional affiliates including MBC Jeju, MBC Chungbuk, MBC Chuncheon, and MBC Daejeon. These affiliates carry shared nationwide MBC programming while also producing their own local news and content for their region.

SBS-affiliated regional broadcasters

SBS follows a similar structure — KBC (Gwangju Broadcasting) serving the Gwangju/Jeonnam region and UBC (Ulsan Broadcasting) serving Ulsan both operate as regional private broadcasters affiliated with the SBS network.

Independently operated regional broadcasters

Not every regional broadcaster is a network affiliate. OBS Gyeonggi is an independent terrestrial broadcaster serving the Gyeonggi/Incheon area, and regional cable broadcasters like KCN (Geumgang) and NIB (Incheon) each provide content tailored to their own local area.

Why regional broadcasters matter

Regional broadcasters cover local, community-level news and information that a nationwide network typically can't focus on in depth. For viewers with ties to or interest in a specific region, they often provide more directly relevant coverage than a national channel would.

No, OBS Gyeonggi is an independent terrestrial broadcaster not affiliated with a specific nationwide network.
